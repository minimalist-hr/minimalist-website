Foundation

Positioning

Minimalist is a software development studio that designs and engineers clear, reliable, and enduring digital products for companies that refuse to compromise on quality.

Principles
  1. Purpose before features.
  2. Engineered to endure.
  3. Clarity at every layer.
  4. Craft without compromise.
  5. Honest partnership.
Identity in one line

One typeface, no colour, generous void. The work is the ornament.

02

Logo system

Two marks, one voice. The wordmark signs; the integrated mark stands in wherever the name has to fit a square. Nothing else is sanctioned.

01  /  Wordmark Primary
MINIMALIST

Tracking −4%, weight 500, closed by a square in the text colour — punctuation, not a symbol. The primary signature everywhere the name can run wide.

02  /  Integrated mark Primary
M INIMALIST

Both elements live inside the square: the M at cap scale, INIMALIST set to exactly the M’s ink width beneath it. Self-contained — never set the full wordmark beside it. Floor 96px in layouts; below that the M square goes alone. Icons are the exception — favicons and app icons carry the full mark down to 48px.

Which mark, where

The wordmark signs anything that can hold a horizontal line of type: site header, letterhead, deck covers, email, contracts, signage.

The integrated mark takes over wherever the layout is square or the name must fit a fixed box: avatars, app icons, favicons, merchandise, stamps on photography.

Never both on one surface. Never the integrated mark beside the wordmark.

The square

Sized at 20% of the wordmark's font size, baseline-aligned, set one optical space after the T. Always the same colour as the lettering.

It is part of the wordmark — never dropped, never tinted away from the text, never used on its own as a mark.

Color

Four neutrals, no accent. The identity carries on contrast and void alone — colour is what every other studio reaches for.

Ink
#111110
Text, marks, inverted fields
Paper
#F5F4F1
Default ground, 80% of surfaces
Graphite
#8A8880
Labels, metadata, captions
Rule
#DCDAD3
Hairlines, dividers, grids
Ratio
Ink and paper hold the layout. Graphite is the only step between them; never introduce a fifth value.
Contrast
Ink on paper 15.8:1, paper on ink the same. Graphite on paper 3.3:1 — labels and captions only, never body copy.
Dark mode
Ink ground, paper text, graphite unchanged. Hairlines drop to #2C2B28.

Photography

Photography is the only place the brand admits texture. It documents — rooms, hands, screens, weather — and never performs. Black and white throughout, so it never competes with the type. The images below are stand-ins for treatment and crop only — the subject brief is opposite.

Subjects to shoot
Workspace — desks mid-work; cables, coffee, screens on.
People — working, not posing. Backs, hands, profiles; no eye contact.
Detail — one object, close, flat light.
Place — the studio and the city it sits in, in plain weather.
Treatment
Greyscale, no exception — no split tone, no warm wash.
Contrast 0.92, brightness 1.04 — flat, document-grade.
Crops: 4:5, 3:2 or 1:1. No rounding, no drop shadow.
Available light only. No flash, no bokeh backgrounds.
Never
Stock abstraction — glowing networks, circuit boards, handshakes.
Screens composited with fake dashboards.
Anyone smiling at the lens in a white-cyc studio.
Colour, gradients or duotone of any kind.
MINIMALIST Less noise. Better software.
Type on image
Setting type over photography

Darken the image to 42% brightness and set paper type at full opacity — never a translucent scrim, never a gradient fade. If the copy still fights the image, the image is wrong.

One line of headline and the mark, nothing more. Body copy always sits on paper or ink, never on a photograph.

Minimum contrast 4.5:1 against the darkest region the type crosses — check the light corner, not the average.

09

Voice & messaging

Rules
  1. Quiet confidence. Never raise the voice.
  2. Short sentences. Precise words.
  3. State the fact, then stop.
  4. No jargon, no hype, no filler.
  5. Never "passionate", "cutting-edge", "solutions-driven".
Say this

"We rebuild fragile systems into clean architecture, preserving everything that already works."

Not this

"A passionate team delivering innovative, cutting-edge solutions that drive real business value at scale."

Boilerplate  /  34 words

Minimalist partners with ambitious companies to create software that performs at the highest level and stays simple at its core. Deliberately small, senior, and focused. No layers, no handoffs, no diluted accountability.

Manifesto  /  the long-form voice

Every product begins with noise. Too many features. Too many opinions. Too many ways to go wrong.

Our work is to remove it.

The best software feels inevitable, as if it could not have been built any other way. That feeling is never an accident. It is the result of disciplined thinking, deliberate design, and engineering that holds up long after launch.

Complexity is easy. Anyone can add.
Clarity is earned. It takes judgment to know what to leave out.

That judgment is what we bring.

Less noise. Better software.

Assets & naming

Master artwork lives in /assets. Never redraw a mark, screenshot one out of this document, or rebuild one in a deck — export from the master or ask for the file. Open the export sheet.

Naming convention
minimalist-mark-colour.ext
mark — wordmark, mark, square
colour — ink or paper, named for the artwork, not the background
ext — svg, png, ico
Lowercase, hyphens only. No version numbers, no dates, no "final". The folder is the version.
Which format
PNG — the default for anyone outside the studio. Renders identically everywhere.
SVG — for editing and for our own sites, where Archivo is already loaded.
ICO — favicon only. One file, six sizes.
Send vector only when the recipient asked for it, and outline the type first. Otherwise the PNG is the safer file.

Before you send a file
Named to the convention — a renamed file is an untracked file.
Correct artwork colour for the ground it lands on, not the one on your screen.
PNG unless the recipient asked for vector.
